# ADR-0025 — The form: repeats, voltas, jumps, bar repeats and pass-specific playing

- **Status:** Accepted (owner's decision, 2026-11-03; SCALETTA F1.10). The owner set the scope:
  the core (repeat barlines, voltas, segno, coda, Fine, D.C./D.S. and their variants) plus bar
  repeats (%), music played only on some passes, and jumps written as free text. After examples of
  the numbering styles, the owner chose one bar-numbering style per document instead of a flag per
  volta (F9), and approved every other recommendation of §11 as written.
- **Date:** 2026-11-03
- **Refines:** ADR-0004 (repeats and jumps are form markings stored once; `playback` unrolls them
  into passes; a played note is (written note, pass index)), ADR-0018 §5 (the bar numbering tied
  to voltas was left to the form), ADR-0022 §3 and §6 (bars have stable `BarId`s; position-anchored
  things sit at (bar, offset)).
- **Amends:** nothing.

## Context

The written score is stored bar after bar, as printed (ADR-0018). What is *played* is the score
unrolled: sections repeated, endings chosen, jumps taken. ADR-0004 says this is derived by
`orchestra-playback` (F1.12) from **form markings stored once**, and that every played note is
identified by (written note ID, pass index), so that editing a note in the second pass of a repeat
edits the written note unless a pass-specific offset is stored (ADR-0024 §10).

This ADR decides what the form markings are, where they live, which states are valid, and the
exact rules F1.12 will follow to unroll them. It does not implement the unrolling (F1.12), the
commands (F1.11) or the engraving (F2B).

### Sources checked (2026-11-03)

- **MusicXML 4.0.** A repeat is a `<repeat direction="forward|backward">` inside a `<barline>`;
  `times` gives how many times a section without endings is played, and `after-jump` (new in
  4.0) whether a repeat is played after a D.C. or D.S.; both apply only to backward repeats that
  are not part of an ending
  ([repeat](https://w3c.github.io/musicxml/musicxml-reference/elements/repeat)). An `<ending>`
  has a `number` that may list several passes ("1, 2") and a `type` of `start`, `stop` (with the
  downward jog, typical of a first ending) or `discontinue` (without it, typical of a last
  ending) ([ending](https://w3c.github.io/musicxml/musicxml-reference/elements/ending)). Jumps are
  `<sound>` attributes: `dacapo`, `dalsegno` and `tocoda` with the name of their target, `fine`;
  "a dalsegno or dacapo attribute indicates that the jump should occur the first time through,
  while a tocoda attribute indicates the jump should occur the second time through", and
  `time-only` restricts a sound to given passes
  ([sound](https://w3c.github.io/musicxml/musicxml-reference/elements/sound)). Bar repeats are a
  `<measure-repeat>` measure style (one, two or more bars).
- **MEI.** Bar repeats are explicit elements: `mRpt` ("the previous measure should be repeated"),
  `mRpt2`, `multiRpt`, and `beatRpt` for beats; a discussion on MEI-L proposed `@copyof` or a
  `cpMark` to say which events are repeated, without a settled answer
  ([mRpt](https://music-encoding.org/guidelines/v3/elements/mrpt.html),
  [beatRpt](https://music-encoding.org/guidelines/dev/mei-basic/elements/beatRpt.html),
  [MEI-L](https://lists.uni-paderborn.de/pipermail/mei-l/2015/001644.html)).
- **Dorico** plays the sections between repeat barlines twice by default, and skips repeats after
  a D.C./D.S. unless "Play repeats after D.S./D.C. jump" is set; users report that only one set of
  endings is honoured after a jump, and that there is no way to say on which pass a D.S. inside a
  repeated section takes effect
  ([help](https://steinberg.help/dorico/v4/en/dorico/topics/play_mode/play_mode_repeats_playback_r.html),
  [forum](https://forums.steinberg.net/t/i-have-a-question-about-how-to-play-repeatedly/1030220),
  [forum](https://forums.steinberg.net/t/repeat-endings-not-repeating-after-d-s-al-fine-in-dorico-3-pro/138929)).
- **Bar numbers in voltas.** Forum summaries of Gould's *Behind Bars* (p. 237) report two systems,
  "155a/155b" and straight-through numbering, the latter recommended for ensemble music; classical
  editions often use the letters; Dorico continues the numbers through the endings by default
  ([notat.io](https://notat.io/viewtopic.php?p=7103),
  [notat.io](https://www.notat.io/viewtopic.php?p=12065),
  [Dorico help](https://www.steinberg.help/dorico/v1/en/dorico/topics/notation_reference/notation_reference_bar_numbers_repeat_endings_c.html)).
- **MuseScore** describes every jump with three labels, "Jump to", "Play until" and "Continue
  at", matched against the labels of markers (segno, coda, fine, start and end of score), plus a
  "Play repeats" option; several jumps and markers are told apart by renaming their labels
  ([handbook](https://handbook.musescore.org/3/notation/repeats-and-jumps)).

What the sources show: every program models jumps as **jump to / play until / continue at**,
with an explicit choice about repeats after the jump; the common failures are ambiguity (which
segno, which pass) and string labels that can go stale. IDs instead of labels, and explicit pass
rules, remove both.

## Decision

All types live in `orchestra-core-model`, next to the bar list. Every form object has its own ID
from the document's counter (ADR-0022 §1): `RepeatId`, `VoltaId`, `MarkerId`, `JumpId`,
`BarRepeatId`, `PassRuleId`. Few per score (tens), so they are vectors sorted by bar.

### 1. Form markings sit on barlines (F1)

A repeat, a volta, a marker or a jump belongs to a **barline**: the start or the end of a bar,
identified by its `BarId`. A repeat in the middle of a written bar (a section that begins after a
pickup beat) is written, as every program prints it, by splitting the bar into two irregular
bars (ADR-0018: an `AlignEnd` bar and an `AlignStart` bar, the second uncounted): no form marking
needs an offset. So unrolling cuts the score only at barlines, and a played pass is always a run
of whole bars, which keeps F1.12 and the DAW's bar ruler simple.

The form lives in the **document**, not in an instrument: a repeat barline crosses the whole
system, every instrument plays the same passes. The only per-staff and per-instrument form
objects are bar repeats (§6) and pass rules (§7).

### 2. Repeats (F2, F3)

```text
RepeatStart { id: RepeatId, bar: BarId }                    // ‖: at the start of `bar`
RepeatEnd   { id: RepeatId, bar: BarId, times: u8 }         // :‖ at the end of `bar`
```

- `times` is the **total** number of plays of the section (2 by default, "3×" = 3), between 2 and
  `MAX_PLAYS` = 64 (a technical limit against corrupt files, like the voice limit of ADR-0022). It
  is ignored when the section has voltas (§3): then the highest volta number decides.
- An end without a start goes back to the **start of the score** (F2), as Dorico and MuseScore do,
  and as players read it. A start without an end is a valid but inert state (it prints, it plays
  nothing twice), so that a half-entered repeat is not an error while the user is still writing.
- Repeated sections **do not nest or overlap** (F3): between a start and its end there is no other
  start or end. Notation cannot show which barlines pair in nested repeats, and neither MusicXML
  nor the programs above define them; `validate` rejects them. Consecutive sections
  (`‖: A :‖‖: B :‖`) are fine.

### 3. Voltas

```text
Volta { id: VoltaId, first: BarId, last: BarId, passes: PassSet, closed: bool,
        text: Option<Box<str>> }
```

- A volta spans `first..=last` and is played on the passes of `passes` (a set of pass numbers
  1–64, a `u64` bit set: "1.–3." is {1, 2, 3}). `closed` draws the downward jog at the end
  (MusicXML's `stop` versus `discontinue`). `text` replaces the printed numbers ("1.–7.", "Last
  time").
- The voltas of one repeated section are consecutive, the last but one ends at the section's
  `RepeatEnd`, and the last may follow it (the usual open second ending). Their pass sets are
  disjoint, and together they cover 1..=n without gaps, where n (the highest number) is the number
  of plays. `validate` checks all of it.
- **Bar numbers** (ADR-0018 §5, F9): one **style per document**, because an edition numbers all
  its voltas the same way:
  - `Continue` (the default): every written bar takes the next number, voltas included, so the
    number is always the written bar, the DAW's bar ruler and "from bar 9" at a rehearsal. Gould
    (*Behind Bars*, p. 237, as summarised in the sources below) recommends it for ensemble music;
    it is Dorico's default.
  - `SameAsFirstLettered`: the bars of every volta take the numbers of the first volta's bars,
    with a letter per volta ("8a", "8b", "8c"), the style of many classical editions and critical
    commentaries.
  - `SameAsFirst`: the same numbers without letters ("8", "8").

  With the two "same as first" styles, the bar after the voltas continues from the **longest**
  volta: volte of two bars and one bar numbered 7a 8a | 7b give 9 after them. Examples, for a
  repeat of bars 1–8 with a one-bar first and second volta:

  ```text
  written:               1 … 7 |1.[ ] :‖|2.[ ] | · ·
  Continue:              1 … 7 |   8    |   9   | 10 11
  SameAsFirstLettered:   1 … 7 |   8a   |   8b  |  9 10
  SameAsFirst:           1 … 7 |   8    |   8   |  9 10
  ```

  The style is stored now because it is score data and changes the file format. The display
  (letters, position) is F2B's.

### 4. Markers and jumps (F4, F5)

```text
Marker { id: MarkerId, bar: BarId, kind: Segno | Coda | Fine | ToCoda, text: Option<Box<str>> }
Jump   { id: JumpId, bar: BarId,                      // at the end of `bar`
         to: Start | Marker(MarkerId),                // a Segno
         until: None | Marker(MarkerId),              // a Fine or a ToCoda
         then: None | Marker(MarkerId),               // the Coda to continue at, after a ToCoda
         repeats: Skip | Play,                        // repeats after the jump (F4)
         fires: AfterRepeats | OnPass(u8),            // when a jump inside a repeat is taken (F5)
         text: Option<Box<str>> }
```

- A segno and a coda mark the **start** of their bar, a Fine and a "To Coda" the **end**.
- A jump is MuseScore's triple with IDs instead of labels: go to the start or to a segno, play
  until a Fine (stop) or a "To Coda" (continue at the coda in `then`), or play on to the end.
  D.C., D.C. al Fine, D.C. al Coda, D.S., D.S. al Fine and D.S. al Coda are combinations of the
  fields; the printed words are derived from them. Several segni and codas (D.S.S., "Coda 2") are
  told apart by their IDs, so nothing can point at the wrong one.
- **Free-text jumps** (owner's scope): `text` replaces the derived words and the fields keep the
  behaviour explicit ("D.C. senza ritornelli" = to the start, repeats played; "D.S. e poi Coda"
  with its own wording). The same `text` field exists on markers and voltas.
- **Each jump is taken once**, the first time it is reached on the path (after any repeat that
  contains it, see F5); a Fine or a "To Coda" acts only after a jump that names it in `until`.
  This makes the unrolled path finite by construction.
- **Repeats after a jump** (F4): by default `Skip`, the classical convention (and MusicXML's
  `after-jump="no"`): every repeated section is played once, and a section with voltas plays only
  its **last** volta. `Play` plays them again in full.
- **A jump inside a repeated section** (F5) is taken by default on the section's **last pass**
  (`AfterRepeats`), which is what a D.S. at the end of a repeated chorus means; `OnPass(n)` takes
  it on pass n, the case Dorico cannot express.

### 5. The unrolling rules F1.12 will follow

1. The path starts at bar 0 with no jump taken, and walks bars in order.
2. At the end of a bar with a `RepeatEnd`, if the section's play count is not reached (and repeats
   are not skipped after a jump), go back to its `RepeatStart` (or the start of the score), count
   one more play.
3. Entering a volta whose pass set does not contain the current play skips to the bar after it;
   with repeats skipped after a jump, only the last volta is entered.
4. At the end of a bar with a jump not yet taken (and on the pass `fires` says), take it: go to
   its target and remember it as the active jump; it is never taken again.
5. With an active jump, the end of its `until` marker stops the path (Fine) or continues at the
   start of its `then` coda (To Coda).
6. The path ends after the last bar.

The **pass index** of a played bar (ADR-0004) is the number of times the same written bar was
played before on the path, from 0: unambiguous, independent of how the repeat was reached, and
the key of the pass-specific offsets of ADR-0024 §10.

### 6. Bar repeats (%) (F6, F7)

```text
BarRepeat { id: BarRepeatId, staff: StaffId, first: BarId, count: u32, span: One | Two | Four }
```

- `count` consecutive bars from `first` repeat, on one **staff** (F7), the `span` bars before
  them (the one-, two- and four-bar repeat signs). Per staff because a piano's left hand may
  repeat while the right hand writes new music.
- The repeated bars **store no events** on that staff (F6): what is played is the source bars,
  derived by F1.12 (the played notes are the source notes with their own IDs, at a later pass of
  the unrolled timeline). Copying the events would be the duplication ADR-0003 forbids: an edit of
  the source would not reach the copies. `validate` checks that the bars hold no event on the
  staff, that the source bars exist (at least `span` bars before), and that two bar repeats of a
  staff do not overlap.
- Beat repeats (a slash repeating a beat) are left out: rare, and they would need offsets.

### 7. Music played only on some passes (F8)

```text
PassRule { id: PassRuleId, start: ScorePoint, end: ScorePoint, scope: DynamicScope,
           plays: PassSet, text: Option<Box<str>> }
```

- A range of one instrument (or one voice, like a dynamic's scope, ADR-0024 §8) played only on
  the passes in `plays`: "tacet 1st time" = {2}, "2nd time only" = {2}, "play 3 times, last only"
  = {3}. The printed text is derived ("1st time tacet") unless `text` replaces it.
- The pass numbers are those of the **repeated section containing the range** (pass 1 = first
  play), the numbers players read above the music. `validate` checks that the range lies inside
  one repeated section and that `plays` is not empty.
- A range rather than a set on every event: in scores this is one instruction over a passage, and
  a range keeps it one object to select, print and edit.

### 8. Validation (rules 8–11 of `Document::validate`)

8. Repeats: no nesting or overlap; `times` within 2–`MAX_PLAYS`.
9. Voltas: inside or right after one repeated section, consecutive, disjoint pass sets covering
   1..=n, the last but one ending at the `RepeatEnd`.
10. Markers and jumps: targets exist and have the right kind (a segno for `to`, a Fine or "To
    Coda" for `until`, a coda for `then`, `then` present exactly with a "To Coda"); `OnPass(n)` at
    most the plays of its section.
11. Bar repeats and pass rules as in §6 and §7.

### 9. Not decided here

The unrolling itself and its incremental update (F1.12), the commands that keep the form valid
when bars are inserted or deleted (F1.11: deleting a bar that carries a form marking deletes the
marking in the same command, as for slurs), the engraving (F2B), the MusicXML mapping (§5 of
SCALETTA). Movements (several pieces in one file) are not modelled; when they come, "the start of
the score" in F2 and in D.C. becomes "the start of the movement".

### 10. Hard cases → tests (F1.10b)

| Case | Test |
|---|---|
| Repeat ×3 without voltas | the section's play count is 3 |
| End repeat without start | goes back to bar 0 |
| First and second volta, open second | coverage {1}, {2}; the last bar of volta 1 carries the end |
| "1.–3." and "4." | pass sets {1, 2, 3} and {4}, four plays |
| D.S. al Coda with a repeat inside the segno section | jump on the last pass; repeat skipped after it, last volta taken |
| D.C. al Fine with `repeats: Play` | the repeat is played again |
| D.S.S. (two segni) | each jump points at its own segno by ID |
| Bar repeat over two bars on the piano's lower staff | the bars hold no events there; overlap rejected |
| "Tacet 1st time" on the trumpet | pass set {2}, inside one repeated section |
| Nested repeats, gaps in volta numbers, a jump to a coda | rejected by `validate` |

### 11. Choices for the owner

| # | Question | Options | Recommended, with its reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Where form markings sit | **barlines only** (mid-bar repeats by splitting the bar); any (bar, offset) | **Barlines only**: what the programs print, passes become runs of whole bars; the split bar is already in ADR-0018 |
| F2 | End repeat without a start | **start of the score**; after the previous end repeat or double barline | **Start of the score**: Dorico, MuseScore and common reading; an explicit start repeat says anything else |
| F3 | Nested repeats | **forbidden**; allowed, innermost pairing | **Forbidden**: notation cannot show the pairing, no format defines it |
| F4 | Repeats after a jump | per jump, **default skip** (last volta taken); always skip; always play | **Per jump, default skip**: the classical convention, MusicXML's `after-jump`, and the explicit choice Dorico lacks |
| F5 | When a jump inside a repeated section is taken | **after the last pass, or on a chosen pass**; always the first pass (MusicXML's literal reading) | **Last pass by default, `OnPass(n)` when written otherwise** |
| F6 | Content of bar-repeat (%) bars | **no events, played from the source**; events copied and drawn as % | **No events**: copying would duplicate data (ADR-0003) |
| F7 | Bar repeats per | **staff**; instrument | **Staff**: one hand may repeat while the other plays |
| F8 | Music played only on some passes | **a range per instrument or voice**; a pass set on every event | **Range**: one instruction over a passage, as written in scores |
| F9 | Bar numbers in later voltas | **continue by default, "same as first volta" as an option stored now**; only continue, decide later | **Option stored now**: it is score data, and adding it later changes the file format. **Owner's decision:** one style per document (`Continue`, `SameAsFirstLettered`, `SameAsFirst`), the bar after the voltas continuing from the longest one |
| F10 | Limit on plays | **64** (a `u64` pass set); 32; none | **64**: far beyond real music (a vamp "repeat 8×"), keeps pass sets in one machine word |

## Alternatives rejected

- **Form inside each instrument:** every instrument would repeat the same markings, and they could
  disagree.
- **Labels (strings) linking jumps to markers** (MuseScore): they go stale when a marker is
  renamed or deleted; IDs cannot point at the wrong thing.
- **A tree of sections** (A, B, repeat(A)…): elegant for unrolling, but it is not what is written,
  and every edit of a barline would restructure the tree. The flat markings are the score, the
  path is derived.
- **Storing the unrolled timeline:** a second copy of the order of bars, forbidden by ADR-0003; it
  is derived in F1.12.

## Consequences

- F1.10b implements §2–§8 in `core-model` (new IDs, the form lists, `PassSet`, the builder methods,
  rules 8–11 of `validate`), with an oracle and allocation-free lookups; F1.12 implements §5.
- The bar numbering of ADR-0018 §5 gains the volta styles (F9): `core-time` keeps the plain
  numbering, the document applies the style chosen for it.
- The file format of F1.15 stores the form lists; the derived path is never saved.
- Deleting or inserting bars must keep the form valid (F1.11).
